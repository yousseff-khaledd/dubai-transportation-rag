# Source-based review of the Qdrant evaluation

This review uses the saved answers and selected chunk text in `results.json`. It does not change reference answers or replace Ragas scores. It is a review of the indexed evidence, not independent verification of current law.

## Cases requiring attention

- **Q02 — source attribution remains a problem.** The answer introduces the requirement as “Under Law No. (9) of 2023” and then adds comprehensive insurance. The cited Law chunk `ad4140c36714b474e0f6` requires a UAE-licensed insurer, as prescribed by the Director General. Comprehensive insurance appears in selected document `1872`, chunk `77e58acfb3acfcd78994`, Article 7 of a separate resolution. Both sources are cited, but the answer should distinguish which document adds which requirement. The same concern appeared in the SQLite baseline. The new faithfulness score is 0.5; the text inspection establishes the attribution concern independently of that score.

- **Q03 — the reference is shorter than the answer.** The answer gives AED 75 and lists four exemptions. Cited chunk `23d885d0bfc09fdf430f`, document `1432`, pages 1–2, contains the fee and all four exemptions. Faithfulness is 1.0 while correctness is about 0.429. The extra detail relative to the short reference is a plausible scoring limitation; the saved cited text supports it.
- **Q04 — apparent faithfulness judge mismatch.** The answer says the fine is 10,000 dirhams. Its cited chunk `d58a829ce70544981735`, document `1915`, page 11, explicitly says “Conducting the Activity without a Permit | Fine: 10,000 dirhams” under Schedule (2). The zero faithfulness score conflicts with that direct passage match, while correctness is about 0.951 and context recall is 1.0. Retain the raw score and flag it for human review rather than treating it as proof the fee was fabricated.

- **Q06 — answer-agent tool limit, not missing evidence.** Both attempts returned `call_limit_exceeded`. Read-only inspection of their SQLite checkpoint tasks identifies `ToolCallLimitExceededError`: five `get_document_excerpt` calls were requested against a limit of four. The expected source chunk `48f24bb9df8edb1d13b7` from document `1739` was ranked first in both attempts. This failure therefore counts as an end-to-end error despite successful source retrieval, and it receives no Ragas scores. The call limits were kept unchanged during evaluation.

- **Q07 — live fallback completed successfully.** The checkpoint records `ranking_stage=retrieval` with nonempty candidates after an invalid reranking response. Chat still answered, with faithfulness 1.0 and correctness about 0.962. Its answer states the toll trigger directly without the extra tag requirement seen in the baseline. Answer relevancy remains about 0.709, so that score alone should not be read as evidence of the previous over-answering problem. One successful fallback case does not establish its general quality impact.

- **Q08 — recovered answer, with scores needing review.** This question failed during reranking in the SQLite baseline. The new answer covers all types of unmanned aircraft and the military-purpose exception with DCAA coordination. Cited chunk `0386faaeae372c54791f` states both. Faithfulness is about 0.667 and correctness about 0.424 despite that direct support; treat these as judge estimates requiring review, not automatic proof that the exception is wrong.

- **Q09 — second successful fallback.** This question also failed in the old run. The new checkpoint records retrieval-order fallback; the answer now states that the vessel owner, operator, or maritime agent needs a permit first. Cited chunk `dcda48e463cf7d003588` states that requirement. The recorded scores are faithfulness 0.75 and correctness about 0.806. This is another successful request after reranker failure, not proof that fallback always preserves answer quality.
- **Q10 — apparent correctness judge mismatch persists.** The answer quotes the taxicab definition in selected chunk `ccb4498ed146b396a8bb`: vehicles designated for transporting passengers for a specific fare. Faithfulness is 1.0, but correctness is about 0.440. As in the old review, direct agreement with the cited definition makes this a case for human review of the judge score.

## Scope of the conclusions

The case questions, references, expected statuses, expected documents/chunks, filters, and modes match the SQLite run. The database implementation and reranker failure behavior changed together, and model outputs can vary. Changes in aggregate scores cannot be attributed solely to Qdrant. The same deployed model judges and generates answers. A small corpus-derived set and short references also limit what perfect context recall establishes. Keep these measurements as a baseline, not a claim of general legal accuracy.
